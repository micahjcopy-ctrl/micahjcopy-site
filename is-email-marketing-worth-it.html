Is email marketing still worth it for a small business?

Short answer: yes, more than almost anything else you can do. But not for the reason every blog repeats. The famous ROI number is inflated, and the real case for email is simpler and more durable than a stat.

THE STAT IS INFLATED YOU OWN THE AUDIENCE IT COMPOUNDS

Every article on this question opens with the same line: email returns $42 for every $1 you spend. You've probably seen it a dozen times. I'm going to do something the other articles won't, which is tell you that number is softer than it looks, and then give you the reason email is still the best use of your time anyway.

Let's be honest about the stat. That $36-to-$42 figure is an industry average, skewed by huge senders with enormous lists, and it's built partly on open and click data that got a lot less reliable once Apple started pre-loading images and inflating opens. So take the exact number with a grain of salt. If a marketer leads with it and nothing else, they're selling you the shiny version.

Here's the part that's actually true, and matters more.

The real reason

Email is the only marketing channel where you own the audience outright. Not rent it. Own it. Everything else, your reach lives at the mercy of a platform that can change the rules, throttle you, or disappear your traffic overnight. Your email list is yours, and that's worth more than any ROI multiple.

Owned beats rented, and it's not close

Think about where else your customers "live." Your social following belongs to the platform, and organic reach there has been quietly strangled for years. Your search traffic belongs to Google, and plenty of businesses have watched a single algorithm change erase most of it overnight. Your ad audience rents you attention by the click, and the moment you stop paying, it's gone.

Your email list is different in kind. You can export it. You can reach every person on it whenever you want, for essentially free, without asking a platform's permission or bidding against a competitor. When something changes, an algorithm, an ad cost, a channel dying, your list is the asset that's still standing. For a small business, that stability is the whole point. You're not building on rented land.

It's cheap in a way nothing else is

Most small businesses run email on fifty to a couple hundred dollars a month of software. Compare that to the rising cost of acquiring the same customer through ads, which climbs every year. Email doesn't have an auction. Sending to a thousand people costs about the same as sending to a hundred. So once you've earned someone onto the list, talking to them again is nearly free, which is exactly why it's the best-performing channel for getting an existing customer to come back.

Where the value actually hides

Here's the nuance the stat-dump articles skip. Not all email is equal. The revenue doesn't come from blasting your whole list a discount every Friday. It comes disproportionately from automated flows, the welcome, the cart reminder, the post-purchase, that fire at the right moment for one person. Industry data consistently shows automated emails earning many times more per send than one-off campaigns. So "is email worth it" really means "is a well-built email system worth it," and the answer there is emphatically yes.

Do it lazily, batch-and-blast with no segmentation or flows, and email underdelivers, then you conclude it "doesn't work." It worked fine. It just wasn't built.

So, worth it for you?

If you sell anything more than once, or to anyone who might buy again, or you'd like to stop renting all your attention from platforms that keep raising the rent, then yes. Email is worth it. Not because of a number on a slide, but because it's the one audience you'll still own when everything else shifts under you. Build the list, build the handful of flows, and it becomes the most reliable revenue you have.
