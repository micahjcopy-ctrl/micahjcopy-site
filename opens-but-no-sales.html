Email & SMS · Diagnosis

Your emails get opened. Nobody buys. Here's why.

Healthy open rates and flat sales is one of the most common problems I see, and almost everyone misdiagnoses it. They go rewrite subject lines. But the open already happened. The subject line did its job. The problem is somewhere else entirely.

THE OPEN PROVED ATTENTION OFFER · TIMING · AUDIENCE DIAGNOSE, DON'T GUESS

If people open and don't buy, resist the urge to touch the subject line. The subject line is the one thing you know is working, because it earned the open. Changing it fixes a problem you don't have. The real gap sits between the open and the purchase, and it's almost always one of three things.

The reframe

An open is a test of your subject line. A sale is a test of your message, your offer, and your timing. Confusing the two sends you optimizing the wrong thing for weeks. Good opens with no sales means the promise got attention and the inside didn't earn the click. Look inside.

Cause 1: the message stays on the surface

The most common version. They open, and inside is a benefit, a product shot, a "shop now," and nothing that moves them. It's pleasant and it's forgettable. It never names the actual problem the reader feels or shows what it's costing them to keep living with it, so there's no reason to act today instead of never.

The fix is to go one layer deeper. Name the specific problem the reader already has in their head. Make the stakes real. Then the click becomes the obvious next step instead of an optional one. You're not writing to inform, you're writing to move a belief. Surface-level copy gets opened and ignored. Copy that meets a real problem gets clicked.

Cause 2: you built an audience for attention, not action

This one stings but it's often the truth. If you grew your list with giveaways, viral freebies, or a discount pop-up that anyone would take, you collected a lot of people who like free things and were never going to buy. They happily open. They were never buyers. You built an audience for attention, and now you're surprised it doesn't act.

Most lists are really three groups: people who buy, people who read but don't buy, and people who never engage at all. If your list skews toward readers and freebie-takers, no email is going to squeeze sales out of people who aren't in the market. The fix isn't a better email, it's better capture, earning subscribers at the moment of real buying intent, and segmenting so you're measuring the buyers separately from the browsers.

Cause 3: the timing is off

People buy when they have a reason, not when it's convenient for your send calendar. Blast the same message to everyone on Tuesday and most of them simply aren't in a buying moment. The emails that convert best aren't broadcasts at all, they're triggered by behavior, and they arrive exactly when the person is thinking about you: right after they abandoned a cart, right when a product they bought would be running low, right after they browsed but didn't buy.

If your entire program is manual campaigns and you have no behavior-triggered flows, you're leaving your best-timed, highest-converting emails unsent. That's not a copy problem either. It's a system that isn't catching people at the moment they'd actually say yes.

How to actually find your cause

Don't guess, isolate it. Look at your click rate first: if opens are high but clicks are also low, it's Cause 1, the message. If clicks are decent but sales still don't follow, the gap moved downstream to your offer or the landing page. If your engaged-buyer segment converts fine but your whole-list sends fall flat, it's Cause 2, you're counting a lot of non-buyers. And if you have no triggered flows running, start there, because that's usually the fastest money in the building.

Opens are the good news. They mean you've got attention, which is the hard part. The sale is a separate, fixable problem, and once you know which of the three you're dealing with, it usually isn't hard to close.

Common questions

Why do my emails get opened but not clicked?

The open means your subject line worked. Low clicks mean the message inside stayed on the surface. It named a benefit but not the real problem the reader feels, so there was no reason to act. Go one layer deeper on the problem and what it's costing them.

Should I rewrite my subject lines if opens are high but sales are low?

No. The subject line is the one thing you know is working, because it earned the open. The gap sits between the open and the purchase. Rewriting subject lines fixes a problem you don't have.

Could the problem be my list itself, not my emails?

Often, yes. If you grew the list with giveaways or a broad discount, you collected people who like free things and were never going to buy. The fix is better capture and segmentation, which is exactly what growing a list that actually buys is about.
